%%%%% Section 3 A Library with Rabbit Holes %%%%%
\section{A Library with Rabbit Holes}\label{sec:framework}

Return to the visitor of Section~\ref{sec:introduction}. What he could name
was little: a title (\textit{Kind of Blue}), its artist (Miles Davis), its
genre (jazz). Each fills a field on a record, and each is denotation. The
rest he carried fills no field: the sound of the instruments, the mood, the
atmosphere---quiet, with feeling in it, colors melting into one another.
That rest is connotation.

From here we simplify. Let the library carry these three descriptors and no
others, and of the three let genre be our facet: its foci---jazz, classical,
and the rest---are the floors. Beyond Miles Davis, then, the visitor holds
genre and nothing more.

He can also reach the contents themselves, and listen. So the \emph{content
    data} is ours as well. Together, the facet and the content data are our
\emph{collection data}. What exploration space can we build from it, so that
he can seek what he is after? That question is the subject of this section.

%%% Subsection 3.1 The Multi-Floor Library %%%%%
\subsection{The Multi-Floor Library with Rabbit
    Holes}\label{subsec:multi_floor_library}

Consider first what the facet alone can build: each floor takes one focus,
i.e., one genre, and holds its works in artist and title order. The visitor
asks for the jazz floor and finds \textit{Kind of Blue} and the other works
of Miles Davis. There his intention runs out. Shelf order tells him nothing
of what a work is like, so he cannot move even along the jazz floor toward
what he is after; on the classical floor he can only take works down at
random.

Suppose now that each floor arranges its works by connotative closeness:
beside any work stand the works that feel like it. The floor then gives each
work a coordinate, and along its coordinates the connotation varies
continuously---a spectrum under his feet. Near \textit{Kind of Blue} he finds
works of similar mood, and walking away he finds the mood changing by degrees.

We do not ask a floor to hold every connotation. We ask for the principal
ones---those that stand next after the denotation of genre, and that most
visitors feel in common: instruments, mood, and their like. We ask two things
more: that one spectrum serve every focus, and that it vary independently of the
facet. One facet fixes such a space, so we call it a \textbf{facet-induced
    connotation space}. For now we postulate one, and leave its existence to
Section~\ref{sec:mathematics}. Every work carries a unique coordinate in it, and
that coordinate already lets him explore the jazz floor.

One coordinate system now covers the whole library. One spectrum serves every
focus, so a coordinate means the same connotation on every floor. From
\textit{Kind of Blue} on the jazz floor, moving to the same coordinate on the
classical floor brings him a work of that same connotation
(Figure~\ref{fig:two_views}\,(a)). His reach therefore runs past genre to the
whole building. He set out holding nothing but \textit{Kind of Blue}, Miles
Davis, and jazz, with no words to carry him further; yet the whole library is
open to him.

This is the \textbf{multi-floor library with rabbit holes} that we propose.
The facet chooses the floor; the facet-induced connotation space chooses
the place on it, by the same rule everywhere. A rabbit hole is
therefore vertical---an elevator standing at one coordinate, open on every
floor. So he can open his own way from Miles Davis to Debussy.

This library meets Bj\"orneborn's~\citeyearpar{Bjorneborn20171053} three
affordances (Section~\ref{subsec:systems}).
\textbf{Diversifiability}: beyond what the facet carries, the whole library
opens to him as a multi-dimensional space.
\textbf{Traversability}: he can traverse that space with intent, choosing what
to hold fixed and what to vary. Walking a floor holds the genre and crosses
connotation; entering a rabbit hole holds the connotation and crosses genre.
\textbf{Sensoriability}: he can play a work, listen, and feel what connotation
its position carries.

Foreseeing is what affords his exploration. Where he stands, he can tell what
the works around him are like; in the direction he moves, he can tell what
awaits him. So he sets out with a clear intent.

%%% Subsection 3.2 Seeking Serendipity in the Library %%%%%
\subsection{Seeking Serendipity in the Library}\label{subsec:seeking}

Let the visitor into this space, seeking as ever ``something like Miles
Davis.'' Suppose that what he seeks lies at $v_\text{seeker}$ in the
facet-induced connotation space. He can neither state nor specify it. He does
not know that $v_\text{seeker}$ exists. What he can do is stand before a work
and feel whether it resonates---whether the work's coordinate lies near his
own. That sense of affinity is his; the library has no access to it.

He begins where anyone begins: with what he can name. He asks for the jazz
floor, and there he walks. Under his feet the coordinate varies continuously;
near Miles Davis the resonance strengthens, and he slows, and stops. Call the
place where he stands $v_\text{stand}$. He could have foreseen all of this: on a
floor, one moves toward more of what one already feels. So far this is seeking,
and the space serves it.

Then he steps into the hole at $v_\text{stand}$. The leap holds
$v_\text{stand}$ fixed and lets the genre change entirely; he surfaces on the
classical floor, and before Debussy the resonance holds. The field's oldest
pairing, \textit{unexpected and relevant}~\citep{Kotkov2016180}, becomes
readable as two coordinates: unexpected in every denotation, relevant in the
one connotation the hole held fixed.

And what he finds is more than a piano piece. On the jazz floor the resonance
might still have been jazz-ness, or Miles-ness; the leap stripped those
accounts away, and what still resonates can only be the coordinate the hole
held. At that moment he discovers something in himself---that
$v_\text{seeker}$ exists. ``\textit{So this is what I was looking for}'': the
moment of serendipity, \textit{a mix of unexpectedness and
    insight}~\citep{Makri2012684}---the unexpectedness of the instant before, the
insight of the instant after.

He explores further, and further exploration turns the encounter into insight.
He can ride the same hole to a third floor, turn back, walk a little and leap
again---the ``unanticipated, anomalous and strategic datum'' of
\citet[p.~506]{Merton1948505}: strategic, because here a finding becomes a plan.
The first leap told him that $v_\text{seeker}$ exists; those that follow tell
him what it is---a structure, unnamed, running across every genre. Serendipity
is not spent in the encounter; it begins there, and the library gives it ground
to run on.

The division of labor is exact. The library measures affinity only between
works; that is its geometry. No library can measure the affinity between $v_\text{stand}$ and the
visitor's own $v_\text{seeker}$, for $v_\text{seeker}$ lives in him. The
space does not contain serendipity; it gives serendipity somewhere to happen.


%%% Subsection 3.3 The Curved Universe %%%%%
\subsection{The Content Data Space: A Curved
    Universe}\label{subsec:multi_galaxy}

Genre is our only facet, so the connotation space must come from the content
data. Let us see how the works lie there.

Content data is high-dimensional, often hundreds of dimensions or more. In that
space the works gather by genre into loose clusters, like galaxies in a universe
(Figure~\ref{fig:two_views}\,(b)). This universe cannot be walked. It has far
too many directions for a visitor to hold in mind, and its axes carry no
meaning: moving along one changes many qualities at once. He can
foresee nothing, and so he cannot explore with intent.

Map the multi-floor library into this space, then, and see what becomes of it
(Figure~\ref{fig:two_views}\,(c)). Each floor becomes a curved surface---a
manifold---approximating one galaxy. Each rabbit hole becomes a curve joining
manifold to manifold.

The content data space is thus a curved universe, and our library lies warped
within it. To walk the library, we must straighten it: floors and rabbit holes
crossing at right angles, each coordinate meaning one thing throughout.

%%% Subsection 3.4: Representation Learning and the Disentanglement %%%%%
\subsection{Representation Learning and the Disentanglement
    Challenge}\label{subsec:disentanglement}

Machine learning studies this problem as \textit{representation
    learning}~\citep{Bengio20131798}. A representation is \textit{disentangled}
when distinct factors of variation occupy separable
directions~\citep{Higgins2017}. We seek a representation of the content data
where denotation and connotation are disentangled.

Constructing such a disentangled representation is a recognized open challenge.
In the unsupervised setting it is provably ill-posed: for any disentangled
solution, infinitely many entangled ones generate the same
observations~\citep{Locatello20197247}. Some \textit{inductive bias}---a
structural assumption about the space---is therefore unavoidable. Our task is to
find the one that yields the facet-induced connotation space, and
Section~\ref{sec:mathematics} finds it in optimal transport.
