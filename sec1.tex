\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

Consider a jazz piece by Miles Davis and a classical work by Debussy.
Categorically and acoustically, they are distant. Yet listeners discover
profound commonalities---contemplative sensibility, evocative atmosphere, an
implicit closeness transcending categorical boundaries. Such discoveries
transform perspective---one sees a door between distant domains---and that
transformation lies at the heart of serendipitous experience.

What we pursue is an information exploration space in which such
doors---doors between distant domains---stand everywhere. Each door joins one
genre or category to another by a commonality that no such name carries. Each
joins things so far apart that, without such a door, only chance would bring
them together. An explorer who opens a door may find a serendipitous
experience; or may find nothing, and the door closes quietly again. Either
way, the space affords the search: a door always beside them, to leap into
another world. We call such doors \textit{rabbit holes}.\footnote{In
    \textit{Alice's Adventures in Wonderland}, Alice follows a rabbit down a hole
    into another world.}

A space equipped with rabbit holes everywhere is not a matter of interface
design, but one of representation. A door can be entered only if it exists in
the space beneath the display---the space in which each work has its place,
and each step leads somewhere one can mean to go. That space is what we call a
\emph{representation}: no interface can surface what it does not already
encode~\cite{Blair1985289,Furnas1987964}. Our aim lies there, before any
interface.

What, then, \emph{is} such a representation? We answer first as a concept.
The original data space---the \textbf{multi-galaxy curved universe}, where
genres form separate galaxies and straight lines in data are not straight
lines in meaning---is recast as the \textbf{multi-floor library}. Each floor
gathers works sharing a named descriptor, genre say, and to walk a floor is
to hold that name fixed while moving through everything the name leaves
unnamed. Between the floors the rabbit holes run straight: passages that hold
that unnamed quality fixed while crossing the names entirely. Curved in the
\emph{entangled} universe, straight in the \emph{disentangled} library, the
passage is one and the same. A rabbit hole leaving Debussy on the classical
floor arrives at Miles Davis on the jazz floor---distant in the names we can
give them, close in what those names cannot.

How is such a library to be given definite form? Our answer is the
\textbf{Optimal Transport Fiber Bundle} (OT-FB): a \textit{disentangled}
representation space in which floors and rabbit holes are geometric objects,
determined by the optimal transport that carries the works of one genre onto
those of another. Mathematics secures the concept---once declared, the bundle
is well-defined and essentially unique. Computation gives it reality---it can
be learned from real data.

The affinity between Miles Davis and Debussy is never put into words in this
paper, and never needs to be. It receives a definite form instead: held as
geometry, as coordinates that do not name, the unnamed remains unnamed and yet
gains a place where it can be felt. This is what the rabbit holes offer:
passages through which an explorer can be led by a resonance that words do not
carry---an exploration in which serendipity has somewhere to happen. Our
contribution is the representation that makes such passage possible---and,
with it, an account of why the felt closeness of serendipity resists reduction
to named attributes, and of what a representation must hold instead.

\paragraph{Paper structure.}
Section~\ref{sec:serendipity} reviews serendipity research---its process
models, the affordances of the physical library, and the classical theory of
relevance and of naming---and asks what an information space must hold if
serendipity is to be sought in it: a \emph{disentangled information
    representation}.
Section~\ref{sec:library_universe_model} presents our vision---the multi-floor
library, the disentangled form of the entangled multi-galaxy curved universe.
Section~\ref{sec:modeling} shows how Optimal Transport Fiber Bundle (OT-FB)
geometry realizes this vision.
Section~\ref{sec:computational} turns the geometry into computation: how the
bundle can be learned from the works themselves and the names they are given.
Section~\ref{sec:demonstration} gives the concept reality: the bundle is
learned from an actual music collection, and the result is opened to the
reader as an explorable system.
Section~\ref{sec:discussion} reflects on what the pursuit revealed---the
necessity of disentanglement, the fitness of OT as an inductive bias, and
what the geometry returns to the theory of relevance.
Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} closes the circle opened by Miles
Davis and Debussy.
