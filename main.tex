\begin{abstract}\noindent
    Serendipity cannot be searched for: no one can name in advance what would
    count as finding it. One can explore instead, as library visitors do.
    This paper asks what an information space must offer if serendipity is to
    be sought, and answers with a concept: a multi-floor library with rabbit
    holes. A facet such as genre names the floors, and an unnamed aspect
    shared by every floor arranges the works on each. A \textit{rabbit hole}
    runs vertically through the same position on every floor, so that users
    may leap from a resonating work into another genre, and there seek
    serendipity.
    %
    We show that such a library exists as geometry. The facet forms a
    \textit{fiber bundle} in the content space: each focus---each genre---is a
    fiber, and optimal transport threads the correspondence across them. The
    resulting \textit{optimal transport fiber bundle} (OT-FB) is essentially
    unique given the facet and the content data. It gives every work two
    coordinates: one in the \textit{denotation space}, which carries the names,
    and one in the \textit{facet-induced connotation space}, which holds what
    the foci share and the names miss. Naming thus reaches its limit and
    becomes the ground for what lies beyond it.
    %
    In this geometry, serendipity is an encounter with a work denotatively
    distant and connotatively close, and the insight it brings is the rabbit
    hole itself. The paper thus builds a library with rabbit holes, and shows
    that a name can reach past itself.
\end{abstract}

\begin{quote}\parindent=0pt\parskip=.5em\noindent
    \begin{center}
        \textbf{Structured Abstract}
    \end{center}
    \textbf{Purpose.}
    Serendipity cannot be searched for: what would count as finding it cannot
    be named in advance. Exploration remains. This paper asks what an
    information space must offer if serendipity is to be sought, and frames it
    as one question: how can naming raise a connotative exploration space that
    naming itself cannot reach?

    \textbf{Design/methodology/approach.}
    We begin with the nameless content space, where works lie by
    how they feel. Then a set of words enters---a \textit{facet}, such as
    genre---and we identify the geometry that meaning takes there.

    \textbf{Findings.}
    In the content space, the facet takes the form of an \textit{optimal
        transport fiber bundle} (OT-FB): a cloth woven of warp and weft,
    essentially unique given the facet and the content data. The warp is the
    \textit{denotation space} the facet defines. Optimal transport then finds
    the weft: the \textit{facet-induced connotation space}, holding what the
    foci share and the names miss. Every work gains a coordinate in each.
    Connotative position follows from how the foci stand as wholes, rather
    than from pairwise similarity. Denotation thus raises the connotation it
    cannot express.

    \textbf{Originality/value.}
    A facet opens a way past its own limit. The names fix the floors, and the
    facet-induced connotation space arranges the works on each, by the same
    rule everywhere. The result is a library with rabbit holes. Users search
    and browse by denotation, as in any library, and at any point may enter a
    rabbit hole, where connotation leads instead. Exploration thus reaches
    past the descriptors by standing on them.
\end{quote}
