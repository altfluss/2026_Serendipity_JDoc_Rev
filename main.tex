\begin{abstract}\noindent
    Serendipity cannot be searched for: no one can name in advance what would
    count as finding it. What one can do is explore, as visitors to a library
    always have. This paper asks what an information space must hold if
    serendipity is to be sought in it, and answers with a concept: a library
    with rabbit holes. Its floors classify works by denotation, genre above
    all; through them run \textit{rabbit holes}, vertical passages that cross
    genres entirely while holding fixed a \textit{connotative} closeness no
    single word carries. An explorer may leap through one into another world
    whenever they wish---and may find a serendipitous encounter there, or may
    not; the finding is their own. Such an exploration
    space is a matter of information \textit{representation}---the layer any
    interface presupposes and none can substitute for. What is needed, then, is
    a \textit{disentangled} representation: one that separates the denotative
    from the connotative. To achieve disentanglement, we introduce
    \textit{optimal transport} as a natural inductive bias: the resulting
    \textbf{Optimal Transport Fiber Bundle (OT-FB)} is well-defined and
    essentially unique among the infinitely many representations that fit the
    same data, with a closed-form embedding learnable from real data. What the
    bundle says about rabbit holes is this: they are determined not by what any
    two works have in common, but by how one genre stands to another as a
    whole---which is what transport, and not pairwise distance, can express.
\end{abstract}

\begin{quote}\parindent=0pt\parskip=.5em\noindent
    \begin{center}
        \textbf{Structured Abstract}
    \end{center}
    \textbf{Purpose.}
    Serendipity cannot be searched for: what would count as finding it cannot
    be named in advance. What remains is exploration. This paper asks what an
    information space must hold if serendipity is to be sought in it---a
    question of information \textit{representation}.

    \textbf{Design/methodology/approach.}
    We propose a concept: a library with \textit{rabbit holes}---a
    \textit{disentangled representation} of the entangled data space.
    Its floors classify works by denotation, genre above all; rabbit holes
    run vertically through the floors, crossing genres entirely while holding
    fixed a connotative closeness no single word carries. Once disentangled,
    an explorer can move within a genre or leap through a rabbit
    hole into another, both by intent.

    \textbf{Findings.}
    Introducing \textit{optimal transport} as a natural bias yields the
    \textit{Optimal Transport Fiber Bundle (OT-FB)}: a definite form,
    essentially unique among the infinitely many representations that fit the
    same data, and learnable from real data. What it says about rabbit holes:
    they are determined not by what any two works share, but by how one
    genre stands to another as a whole---which transport, not
    pairwise distance, can express.

    \textbf{Originality/value.}
    The paper locates the design of an exploration space in representation
    rather than interface; realizes that representation in a form both unique
    and learnable from data; and shows that a commonality between distant
    genres is found not pairwise but in how whole domains stand to one another.
    For information science, it marks a limit of naming, which attaches to
    individual works.
\end{quote}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:}
