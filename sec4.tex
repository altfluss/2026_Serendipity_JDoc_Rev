\section{The Definite Form: From Fiber Bundles to Optimal
  Transport}\label{sec:mathematics}

Section~\ref{sec:framework} closed with three questions; this
section answers them. Section~\ref{subsec:library_as_bundle} first
restates the two views of Figure~\ref{fig:two_views} as a single
mathematical object, in whose vocabulary the three can be put
exactly, and gives the short answers.
Section~\ref{subsec:two_genres} then
earns them: first between two genres, then among all of them, where
the object receives its full name.
Section~\ref{subsec:bundle_reach} closes with what the object can,
and cannot, express.


%%% Subsection 4.1 The Library as a Fiber Bundle %%%%%
\subsection{The Library as a Fiber
    Bundle}\label{subsec:library_as_bundle}

% --- (1) Setup: content data + genre label → coordinate pair ---
Let us restate what Section~\ref{sec:framework} drew. The works arrive as content
data: each work $n$ is a point $\mathbf{x}_n \in \mathcal{X} = \mathbb{R}^D$ in
the universe of Section~\ref{subsec:multi_galaxy}, with its genre label $c_n$ as
descriptor. What the library gives each work $n$ is a pair of coordinates $(u_n,
    v_n)$: a denotative coordinate $u_n\equiv u_{c_n} \in \mathcal{U}$---which
floor, its genre-ness---and a connotative coordinate $v_n \in
    \mathcal{V}$---where on the floor.

% --- (2) Φ: floors and rabbit holes as two ways of walking; naming: fiber bundle ---
The library and the universe are joined by an embedding map
\[
    \Phi: \mathcal{U} \times \mathcal{V} \to \mathcal{X},
\]
which carries every point of the library into the universe---not
only the points where works sit, but every point a visitor may walk
(Figure~\ref{fig:library_universe}). The structures of
Section~\ref{sec:framework} are now two ways of walking $\Phi$. Hold
the genre and vary the connotation: $\Phi(u_c, \cdot)$ traces the
floor of genre $c$---its galaxy, which we model, as a working
assumption, as a smooth low-dimensional manifold. Hold the
connotation and vary the genre: $\Phi(\cdot, v)$ traces a rabbit
hole---a curve threading every galaxy through the points that share
$v$. A structure of exactly this kind---a family of manifolds bound
together by a bundle of curves---has a ready-made name in
mathematics: a \textit{fiber bundle}, with the denotation space
$\mathcal{U}$ as its base and the connotation space $\mathcal{V}$ as
its fiber. The library and the universe of
Figure~\ref{fig:two_views} are this one bundle seen twice: flat in
$\mathcal{U} \times \mathcal{V}$, where floors stack and holes run
straight; curved in $\mathcal{X}$, where galaxies scatter and holes
bend.

% --- (3) Three questions: modelable? unique? does v express connotation? ---
The three questions of Section~\ref{sec:framework} can now be put
exactly. First, can a given collection be modeled
by this geometry at all---does such a bundle exist over the works?
Second, if it exists, is it unique? Third, if it were made unique,
could we expect its coordinate $v$ to express connotation?

% --- (4) Answers(予告程度。詳述は 4.2 P2 が担う)---
The answers, in order: yes, it exists---too readily, for any
smooth pairing of galaxy with galaxy yields a bundle that fits the
data; no, it is not unique---singling one out requires a further
principle, an \textit{inductive bias}; and the bundle will not
express connotation of its own accord---the bias must be chosen so
that it does. Both burdens, uniqueness and meaning, thus fall on
the bias. The next section chooses it.

%%% Subsection 4.2 The Optimal Transport Fiber Bundle %%%%%
\subsection{The Optimal Transport Fiber Bundle}\label{subsec:two_genres}

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.6\linewidth]{fig/fig3.pdf}
    \caption{
        \textbf{The two-genre case.}
        Jazz (blue) and classical (gray) as two galaxies in the
        data space, each modeled as a manifold. The gray lines show
        the correspondence singled out by optimal transport---the
        pairing of the two galaxies with least total squared
        distance. Under it, Miles Davis's piece (orange) is paired
        with Debussy's (red): the red line is the rabbit hole
        between the two genres.
    }
    \label{fig:two_category_ot}
\end{figure}

% --- P1: Fig.3 で問題の難しさを直感的に(JDoc/LIS 読者向け)---
\paragraph{The two-genre case.}
Consider the simplest library: two genres only. Figure~\ref{fig:two_category_ot}
shows jazz and classical as the data give them---two galaxies far apart in
$\mathcal{X}$, each modeled as a manifold. On one sits Miles Davis's piece; on
the other, Debussy's. Even as content data, the two are nowhere near each other.
The problem we must solve is to find the lines that join the two
galaxies---not this one line, but all of them.

Drawn together, the lines form a correspondence: a rule laying one galaxy onto
the other. Finding such a correspondence is known to be severely ill-posed
(Section~\ref{subsec:disentanglement}): infinitely many solutions exist, all
fitting the data equally well~\cite{Locatello20197247}. To resolve
it, we must state which solution we count as good---the inductive bias
Section~\ref{subsec:library_as_bundle} called for. In other words, we must
define what we want the connotation coordinate $v$ to express. Fortunately, two
assumptions suggest themselves naturally.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{(A1) Independence:} the connotation we want is
          information independent of the denotation---the
          rabbit hole as the library drew it, a passage running
          perpendicular to the floors.
    \item \textbf{(A2) Proximity:} works sharing a connotation lie
          closer in the data space than works that do not---Miles
          Davis's piece lies nearer to Debussy's than to a
          Beethoven symphony.
\end{itemize}

% --- P3: (A1)+(A2) → OT への帰着(一度だけ)---
Together, the two assumptions turn the search for rabbit holes
into a well-posed problem mathematics already knows: lay one galaxy onto the
other as closely as possible (A2) while keeping the match
independent of genre (A1). This is \textit{optimal
    transport}---carrying all the stars of one galaxy onto the other
with the least total effort, measured by squared Euclidean
distance
(2-Wasserstein)~\cite{villani2009optimal,peyre2019computational}.
For this cost, the correspondence exists and, under mild
regularity conditions, is essentially unique---a classical result
known as Brenier's theorem~\cite{Brenier1991375,villani2009optimal}.

% --- P4: 知見(最近傍では A1 が破れる → 相同性)---
This reformulation offers an insight. Miles Davis and Debussy are not
matched because each is the other's nearest work---matching
nearest works, pair by pair, would not even guarantee (A1). They
are matched because the relative position each occupies within
its own galaxy is homologous
(Figure~\ref{fig:two_category_ot}). A rabbit hole is decided not
by the pairwise relation of two works but by how one genre stands
to another as a whole.

% --- Bridge: 2ジャンル → K ジャンル ---
This settles the library with two floors. A real collection has
many, and correspondences drawn pair by pair need not agree with
one another.

% --- MMOT への拡張(一つの問題に束ねる)---
\paragraph{The $K$-genre case.}\label{subsec:OTFB-model}
The extension is direct. Optimal transport generalizes from two
distributions to $K$ as \textit{multi-marginal optimal
    transport}~\cite{pass2015multi}: a single correspondence threads
all $K$ galaxies at once, picking one point from each and
minimizing the total squared distance among them. Because all
genres now enter one problem, the threads cannot disagree with one
another; and existence and essential uniqueness carry over under
the same kind of regularity conditions~\cite{pass2015multi}.

% --- P2: OT-FB の命名 → Figure 2 に帰結して終わり ---
What this builds is the object
Section~\ref{subsec:library_as_bundle} described. Works on one
thread share one connotative coordinate $v$; with genre-ness
treated as continuous---unobserved genres filling between the
observed ones---each thread becomes a smooth curve through every
galaxy. Galaxies as manifolds, bound by a bundle of such curves:
the fiber bundle of Section~\ref{subsec:library_as_bundle}, its
correspondence no longer arbitrary but optimal. We call this
structure the \textbf{optimal transport fiber bundle
    (OT-FB)}---and it is exactly what
Figure~\ref{fig:library_universe} has been showing all along.

% 見出しは仮(2026-08-02)
\subsection{What the Bundle Can Express}\label{subsec:bundle_reach}

% --- P1: 三つの限定(A1 由来・A2=データ計量由来・V の次元)→ PCA の比喩(一度だけ)---
The OT-FB does not express every connotation. What it expresses is
what the two assumptions define---no more. By (A1), it holds only
connotations independent of denotation: whatever in a work depends
on its genre is left with the genre. By (A2), it depends on the
content data and their metric: a connotation the data do not contain
cannot be found. And the dimension of
$\mathcal{V}$ bounds how much is held at once: a low-dimensional
$\mathcal{V}$ keeps only the principal share of what remains.
What the bundle performs might, as it were, be called a
\textit{principal connotation analysis}: like principal
component analysis, it does not express everything; it returns
coordinates, not names; and it inherits the metric the data carry.

% --- P3: multi-view の開口(限定を肯定形で閉じる)---
It is worth noting that the connotation coordinate is determined
by the content data: another content dataset yields another connotation
coordinate. If music is given in two views, as audio and as
lyrics, the two views open two kinds of rabbit holes---an audio
connotation and a lyrics connotation---and each work receives the
coordinates $(u, v_{\mathrm{audio}}, v_{\mathrm{lyrics}})$. The
library has more holes than any one bundle draws.

% --- P4: sec4 の締め → sec5 へ(短く)---
The OT-FB, then, exists and is essentially unique---in theory.
Whether it can be learned from an actual collection is the
subject of Section~\ref{sec:computation}.
