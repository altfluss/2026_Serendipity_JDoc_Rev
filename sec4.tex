\section{Geometrical Form: The Principal Connotative Space}\label{sec:mathematics}

So far, denotation came first, and connotation lay beyond it. Now we reverse
the view. We begin with the nameless world of content data, and watch what a
name does there. One name is enough: a single facet. Its task here is to draw
out what description leaves behind. From it the space of the unnamed follows,
and we define that space in geometry, not in words.

%%% Subsection 4.1 %%%
\subsection{Content Space, Quale, and Qualia
    Spectrum}\label{subsec:content_space}

\paragraph{Ideal content space.}
As thermodynamics builds on the ideal gas, we build on the \textit{ideal
    content space} $\mathcal{X}$: an idealization of real content data. The ideal
gas averages how molecules move; $\mathcal{X}$ averages how people feel a
work. It is a continuous vector space, and every work that could be made
holds a unique position $x \in \mathcal{X}$.

A point of $\mathcal{X}$ carries the unique quality of one work, so we call a
point a \textit{quality}. Qualities also share aspects---jazz-ness, or a
quiet mood. We borrow a word for such a shared aspect: a \textit{quale}. A
quality is thus the whole of one work; a quale is one aspect running across
many works. Three axioms follow, and the third tells how a quale lies in
$\mathcal{X}$.

\begin{description}
    \item[\textbf{(Axiom 1) Sufficiency.}] The quality of a work carries what
          people feel in it---as far as the connotation we mean to reach.
    \item[\textbf{(Axiom 2) Similarity.}] $\mathcal{X}$ carries an inner
          product $\langle x, x' \rangle$, and closeness in it means likeness
          as people judge it, typically
          $\operatorname{Ave}\bigl[\operatorname{Sim}(w, w')\bigr] =
              \exp \langle x, x' \rangle$~\citep{Shepard19871317}.
    \item[\textbf{(Axiom 3) Manifold hypothesis.}] A quale is a probability
          density over $\mathcal{X}$, characterized by a smooth manifold.
\end{description}

\noindent
Axiom 3 turns sharing into a matter of degree. The density peaks on the manifold
and decays away from it: a work may carry a quale strongly, weakly, or hardly at
all. The manifold is the geometry of the quale, and we call it the \textit{quale
    manifold}---an idealized principal manifold, carrying the dominant structure of
the density and smooth enough to move along.

The three axioms together ask for one thing: a representation space faithful
to what people feel in a work. How that feeling is measured, and how such a
space is obtained, we leave open. We take it as given.

Current practice already reaches for such a space. The manifold hypothesis of
Axiom 3 has served machine learning for years~\citep{Bengio20131798}, and
modern embedding methods---Transformers and large language models---aim at
the very representation these axioms describe~\citep{Grand2022}.


\paragraph{Qualia spectrum.}
A quale can vary continuously---violet shades into blue, blue into green, and so
on to red---and its quale manifold moves with it. We call such a family of
manifolds a \textit{qualia spectrum}. Geometry already knows this structure as a
\textit{fiber bundle}: the base is the parameter---of any dimension---along
which the quale varies, and each fiber is a quale manifold.

\paragraph{Principal common qualia.}
Given a qualia spectrum, another qualia spectrum can be induced. Call the
first the \textit{base spectrum}, since it is the base of a fiber bundle. Give
every manifold in it one common coordinate system, and join the points that
share a value. A second spectrum appears, crossing the first: the
\textit{induced spectrum}, induced by the coordinate system we chose. The two
run as warp and weft, and the quality set is the cloth they weave. We call
what the induced spectrum carries the \textit{common qualia} of the base
spectrum.

The coordinate system can be chosen in infinitely many ways, and most of them
come out entangled. So we choose by distance. Each quale is $n$-dimensional,
and in $\mathcal{X}$ we know $n$; real data do not afford us that, for noise
enters. So fix the coordinate system at $m \le n$ dimensions. Every manifold
of the base spectrum then reduces to $m$, the induced spectrum reduces with
it, and what falls outside counts as noise. Among all such choices, take the
one whose threads run shortest in total: its common qualia are the
\textit{$m$-principal common qualia}. Coordinates and reduction settle in a
single stroke, for choosing either first makes the threads longer than they
need be.

%%% Subsection 4.2 %%%
\subsection{Content Space Equipped with
    Denotation}\label{subsec:denotation}

\paragraph{Denotation and connotation.}
Section~\ref{subsec:naming} divided meaning by naming. That division now falls
on qualia. A quale that has a name is a \textit{denotation}; a quale that has
none is a \textit{connotation}. Each is a manifold in $\mathcal{X}$: a
\textit{denotative manifold}, a \textit{connotative manifold}.

Naming leaves the geometry untouched. Jazz-ness and ``quiet, with feeling in
it'' are manifolds of one kind, and only one of them has a word. The catalogue
records the first; $\mathcal{X}$ holds both.

\paragraph{Facet.}
A spectrum that has a name is a \textit{facet}. Genre is one: its manifolds
are jazz, classical, and the rest, and together they vary with genre-ness.
Facet analysis has long organized catalogues this
way~\citep{Cutter1876,Broughton200649}; here the facet becomes continuous, and
its parameter serves as a coordinate.


%%% Subsection 4.3 %%%
\subsection{Principal Connotative Space}\label{subsec:principal_connotative}
\paragraph{Definition of principal connotative space.}
Let the facet be genre. It gives a fiber bundle: genre-ness as base, the
manifold of each genre as fiber. Now take a spectrum orthogonal to it. Each
of its manifolds crosses every genre, one point in each---a section of the
bundle, and the rabbit hole of Section~\ref{sec:framework}.

By orthogonality the region factors, and every work sits at a pair: its
genre-ness $u$, and the section $v$ it lies on. Fix a dimension
$L_\mathcal{V}$ and take the principal manifold of this product. The
resulting space of sections is the \textbf{principal connotative space}
$\mathcal{V}$---the space of rabbit holes.

\paragraph{Natural inductive bias.}
Infinitely many spectra run orthogonal to a facet, and the data alone cannot
choose among them: the ill-posedness of
Section~\ref{subsec:disentanglement}~\citep{Locatello20197247}. Two
requirements settle it, and both are already ours.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{(A1) Orthogonality.} The spectrum varies independently of
          the facet.
    \item \textbf{(A2) Proximity.} The works on one section lie as close to
          one another as they can.
\end{itemize}

Together these state a problem mathematics already knows. Lay each genre
manifold onto the others as closely as possible, while keeping the match
independent of genre: this is \textit{multi-marginal optimal
    transport}~\citep{villani2009optimal,pass2015multi}. Its solution exists
and, under mild regularity, is essentially unique---Brenier's theorem for two
genres~\citep{Brenier1991375}, its multi-marginal extension for
$K$~\citep{GangboSwiech199823}. We call the bundle so fixed the
\textbf{optimal transport fiber bundle (OT-FB)}, and
Appendix~\ref{sec:computation} gives the proof and the algorithm.

Note what decides a section. Miles Davis and Debussy are joined not because
each is the other's nearest work, but because each holds a homologous
position within its own genre. A rabbit hole follows from how the genres
stand as wholes.

\paragraph{What $\mathcal{V}$ holds.}
The construction has already said what $\mathcal{V}$ is: what remains of
$\mathcal{X}$ once the facet is set aside, reduced to its principal
$L_\mathcal{V}$ components. \textit{Principal} carries its usual sense, as in
PCA. And $L_\mathcal{V}$ is ours to fix---two dimensions serve exploration
well, and more data allow more.

$\mathcal{V}$ is not connotation itself. A connotation moves: it shifts with
the person, the hour, the occasion. No space can be given to what moves---only
the space it moves in, and $\mathcal{V}$ is that space. Every unnamed quale of
the collection lies somewhere in it. So $v$ is not a connotation but a
vocabulary without words, in which one may say: ``\textit{ah, that is
    it---that is how I feel today}.''

One remark. The content data decide $\mathcal{V}$, so a second view of the
same works gives a second $\mathcal{V}$. Take music as sound and as lyrics:
two bundles rise over one facet, and each work receives $(u,
    v_\mathrm{audio}, v_\mathrm{lyrics})$. Two kinds of rabbit hole open
underfoot, and the visitor chooses which to take.
