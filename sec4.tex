\section{Geometrical Form: The Facet-induced Connotation Space}\label{sec:mathematics}

So far, denotation came first, and connotation lay beyond it. Now we reverse the
view. We begin with the nameless world of content data, and watch what a name
does there. One name is enough: a single facet. Its task here is to draw out
what description leaves behind. From that one name the space of the unnamed
follows, and we define that space in geometry, not in words.


%%% Subsection 4.1 %%%
\subsection{Content Space, Quale, and Qualia
    Spectrum}\label{subsec:content_space}

\paragraph{Ideal content space.}
As thermodynamics builds on the ideal gas, we build on the \textit{ideal
    content space} $\mathcal{X}$: an idealization of real content data. The ideal
gas averages how molecules move; $\mathcal{X}$ averages how people feel a
work. It is a continuous vector space, and every work that could be made
holds a unique position $x \in \mathcal{X}$.

A point of $\mathcal{X}$ carries the unique quality of one work, so we call a
point a \textit{quality}. Qualities also share aspects---jazz-ness, or a
quiet mood. We borrow a word for such a shared aspect: a \textit{quale}. A
quality is thus the whole of one work; a quale is one aspect running across
many works. Three axioms follow, and the third tells how a quale lies in
$\mathcal{X}$.

\begin{description}
    \item[\textbf{(Axiom 1) Sufficiency.}] The quality of a work carries what
          people feel in it---as far as the connotation we mean to reach.
    \item[\textbf{(Axiom 2) Similarity.}] $\mathcal{X}$ carries an inner
          product $\langle x, x' \rangle$, and it grows monotonically
          with the likeness people judge between the two
          works~\citep{Shepard19871317}.
    \item[\textbf{(Axiom 3) Manifold hypothesis.}] A quale is a probability
          density over $\mathcal{X}$, characterized by a smooth manifold.
\end{description}

\noindent
Axiom 3 turns sharing into a matter of degree. The density peaks on the manifold
and decays away from it: a work may carry a quale strongly, weakly, or hardly at
all. The manifold is the geometry of the quale, and we call it the \textit{quale
    manifold}---an idealized principal manifold, carrying the dominant structure of
the density and smooth enough to move along.

The three axioms together ask for one thing: a representation space faithful to
what people feel in a work. We leave open how that feeling is measured, and how
such a space is obtained. We take it as given.

Current practice already reaches for such a space. The manifold hypothesis of
Axiom 3 has served machine learning for years~\citep{Bengio20131798}, and the
geometry of learned embeddings already carries how people judge
things~\citep{Grand2022975}.

\paragraph{Qualia spectrum.}
A quale can vary continuously---violet shades into blue, blue into green, and so
on to red---and its quale manifold moves with it. We call such a family of
manifolds a \textit{qualia spectrum} (Figure~\ref{fig:spectra}(a)). Geometry
already knows this structure as a \textit{fiber bundle}, and we call it a
\textit{spectrum fiber bundle}: the base space $\mathcal{U}$ is the
parameter---of any dimension---along which the quale varies, and each fiber is a
quale manifold.

\paragraph{Induced qualia spectrum.}
A spectrum fiber bundle yields a second spectrum. Call the given one the
\textit{base qualia spectrum}. Its fibers share a shape, so a single
coordinate system $\mathcal{V}$ fits them all, and every point of the bundle
gains an address $(u, v)$ (Figure~\ref{fig:spectra}).

Now fix $v$ and let $u$ run. This joins the points at $v$, one from every
fiber, into a manifold of its own---a \textit{section} of the bundle. Let $v$
vary, and the sections form a family. That family is itself a qualia
spectrum, brought forth by the base: the \textit{induced qualia spectrum}.
Its base space is $\mathcal{V}$, and each section is a fiber of it.

The two spectra weave one cloth, and the bundle is their product manifold
$\mathcal{U} \times \mathcal{V}$. The fibers of the base run one way, indexed
by $u$, and they are the warp: the loom holds them already. The sections run
across, indexed by $v$, and they are the weft: we thread them one by one. The
qualities are the cloth.

The warp is given, then, and the weft is ours to choose. Infinitely many
threadings fit, and the data alone cannot choose among them (panel (b)): the
ill-posedness of Section~\ref{subsec:disentanglement}~\citep{Locatello20197247}.
We need an inductive bias. Ours has two parts.

\begin{description}
    \item[\textbf{(Bias 1) Independence.}] The density over the bundle
          factorizes: $p(u, v) = p(u)\,p(v)$.
    \item[\textbf{(Bias 2) Shortest weft.}] Among such coordinate systems,
          take the one whose sections run shortest in total.
\end{description}

\noindent
Bias 1 makes a quale common: $v$ says the same thing wherever it sits on the
base. Bias 2 makes it faithful: by Axiom 2, a short weft joins the qualities
that feel most alike, so each section is spun from like qualities throughout.
The two together form one inductive bias, and we call it the \textit{optimal
    transport bias} (OT bias), since shortest total length is the problem of optimal
transport. The OT bias leaves one bundle: disentangled by Bias 1, taut by
Bias 2. We call that bundle an \textit{optimal transport fiber bundle} (OT-FB),
and its induced spectrum carries the \textit{common qualia} of the base
(panel (c)). Keep $\mathcal{V}$ low-dimensional, and it holds the principal
common qualia---those with least error, as in PCA{}. Finally, we obtain the
disentangled representation (panel (d)).

\subsection{Content Space Equipped with
    Denotation}\label{subsec:denotation}

So far we have seen the geometry of $\mathcal{X}$: a world continuous,
countless, and nameless. But the library is built of names: a world discrete,
few, and spoken. This section joins the two worlds.

\paragraph{Denotation and connotation.}
Section~\ref{subsec:naming} divided meaning by naming. That division now applies
to qualia. A quale with a name is a \textit{denotation}; a quale without one is
a \textit{connotation}. A catalogue, though, names classes, not qualia: jazz is
a set of works, jazz-ness a density over $\mathcal{X}$. In the ideal content
space the two agree: the works of jazz, each a point of $\mathcal{X}$, are drawn
from the density jazz-ness. So the name passes from the class to the quale.

Naming leaves the geometry untouched. Jazz-ness and ``quiet, with feeling in
it'' are alike in kind: each is a quale, a density over $\mathcal{X}$ modeled by
a manifold. Only one of them has a word. The catalogue records the first;
$\mathcal{X}$ holds both.

\paragraph{Facet.}
One characteristic of division yields a group of \textit{foci}, and that group
is a \textit{facet}~\citep{Vickery1960}. Genre is our facet throughout this
paper, and jazz, classical, rock, and the rest are its foci. Each focus is a
named class, and its name passes to a quale. So in $\mathcal{X}$ the facet is a
set of qualia: jazz-ness, classical-ness, rock-ness, and the rest.

One characteristic yields them all, so lay this set along a coordinate $u\in
    \mathcal{U}$. Move $u$, and the quale moves with it: folk-ness shades into
rock-ness, rock-ness into electronic-ness. The facet is then a qualia spectrum
in $\mathcal{X}$, and we call it the \textit{facet spectrum}.

Section~\ref{subsec:principal_connotative} settles which $u$ each focus takes.
For now we suppose it given.

\subsection{Facet-Induced Connotation Space}\label{subsec:principal_connotative}

\paragraph{Definition.}
The facet spectrum is a spectrum fiber bundle, so the construction of
Section~\ref{subsec:content_space} applies unchanged
(Figure~\ref{fig:spectra}). The foci are its fibers, the warp already strung;
thread the weft by the OT bias, and the induced spectrum follows. Every work
then sits at an address $(u, v)$: $u$ says where among the foci, $v$ says which
section.

\paragraph{Facet-induced OT-FB.}
We call the bundle so built the \textit{facet-induced OT-FB}. It is the
multi-floor library with rabbit holes, now in geometry:
Section~\ref{sec:framework} postulated such a library; here it exists. Three
vocabularies now name one structure.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Fix $u$: a fiber appears---one focus, one warp thread, one floor of
          the library. The coordinate $u$ runs over $\mathcal{U}$, from floor
          to floor.
    \item Fix $v$: a section appears---one weft thread, one rabbit hole. The
          coordinate $v$ runs over $\mathcal{V}$, across each floor alike.
\end{itemize}

Figure~\ref{fig:two_views}(c) draws the bundle twice. On the left stands the
library, where a work has an address $(u, v)$: $u$ says which floor, $v$ says
where on the floor. On the right lies the content data, where an embedding
$\Phi$ sends the address to a point $\mathbf{x} = \Phi(u, v)$. Here the two
coordinates curve together: each floor $\Phi(u, \cdot)$ becomes a galaxy, and
each rabbit hole $\Phi(\cdot, v)$ a curve running from galaxy to galaxy. One
representation, two views.

The OT bias does one thing more. Shortening the weft also spaces the warp: it
gives each focus its own $u$. Section~\ref{subsec:denotation} supposed $u$
given; the OT bias supplies it. Fix the facet and the dimensions
$D_\mathcal{U}$ and $D_\mathcal{V}$---the only choices left to us---and the OT
bias leaves one bundle, essentially unique under mild regularity.
Appendix~\ref{sec:computation} gives the proof and the algorithm.

\paragraph{What $\mathcal{U}$ and $\mathcal{V}$ hold.}
The facet has a name, so $u$ carries a denotation: $\mathcal{U}$ is the
\textit{denotation space}. Genre names jazz and classical, nothing more. Yet
the bundle returns two things the names never said.

The first lies between the names. In $\mathcal{U}$, folk-ness sits near
rock-ness, rock-ness near electronic-ness. The foci are usually few, and two
dimensions hold ten genres with room to spare. Give the facet many foci,
though, and $\mathcal{U}$ becomes a space to explore in its own right: let the
facet be artist, and $\mathcal{U}$ places artists by transport cost, so that
users may walk from artist to artist, as well as from floor to floor.

The second lies beyond the names. The coordinate $v$ carries a quale common
to every genre, one the facet cannot name. A quale without a name is a
connotation (Section~\ref{subsec:denotation}), so $\mathcal{V}$ is the
\textit{facet-induced connotation space}. Keep $D_\mathcal{V}$ low, and
$\mathcal{V}$ holds what the foci share with least error: the
\textit{principal connotation space}. $D_\mathcal{V} = 2$ is the usual
answer, since a space one can walk is a plane.

\paragraph{What the correspondence means.}
That shared quale is a correspondence among the genres. Suppose each genre
holds a thousand works. The OT bias then matches every jazz piece to one
classical piece, one folk piece, and so on through every floor---a thousand
matched sets, chosen so that the total distance is least. Draw those matches in
$\mathcal{X}$, and a thousand near-parallel curves appear
(Figures~\ref{fig:spectra}(c) and~\ref{fig:mtg_otfb}(d)). That parallel
structure is the unnamed quale the genres share, and $v$ is the address of one
curve within it.

Say the match sends Miles Davis to Debussy. Homologous position joins them, not
nearness in $\mathcal{X}$: Miles Davis holds a position within jazz, Debussy the
matching position within classical. A rabbit hole rises from how the genres
stand as wholes, rather than from any affinity between two works.

\paragraph{Both worlds, and what follows.}
Both worlds were needed. The connotation came out of the content data, yet the
content data alone could not have yielded it; nor could the facet alone, for a
name gathers only what it names. The name laid a foundation, and the foundation
points toward what it cannot itself represent. Along that direction, we look
into the content data, and the library rises there. Naming reaches its limit,
and there it becomes the ground for what lies beyond.
