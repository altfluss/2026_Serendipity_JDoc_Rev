\section{Geometrical Form: The Facet-induced Connotation Space}\label{sec:mathematics}

So far, denotation came first, and connotation lay beyond it. Now we reverse
the view. We begin with the nameless world of content data, and watch what a
name does there. One name is enough: a single facet. Its task here is to draw
out what description leaves behind. From it the space of the unnamed follows,
and we define that space in geometry, not in words.


%%% Subsection 4.1 %%%
\subsection{Content Space, Quale, and Qualia
    Spectrum}\label{subsec:content_space}

\paragraph{Ideal content space.}
As thermodynamics builds on the ideal gas, we build on the \textit{ideal
    content space} $\mathcal{X}$: an idealization of real content data. The ideal
gas averages how molecules move; $\mathcal{X}$ averages how people feel a
work. It is a continuous vector space, and every work that could be made
holds a unique position $x \in \mathcal{X}$.

A point of $\mathcal{X}$ carries the unique quality of one work, so we call a
point a \textit{quality}. Qualities also share aspects---jazz-ness, or a
quiet mood. We borrow a word for such a shared aspect: a \textit{quale}. A
quality is thus the whole of one work; a quale is one aspect running across
many works. Three axioms follow, and the third tells how a quale lies in
$\mathcal{X}$.

\begin{description}
    \item[\textbf{(Axiom 1) Sufficiency.}] The quality of a work carries what
          people feel in it---as far as the connotation we mean to reach.
    \item[\textbf{(Axiom 2) Similarity.}] $\mathcal{X}$ carries an inner
          product $\langle x, x' \rangle$, and closeness in it means likeness
          as people judge it, typically
          $\operatorname{Ave}\bigl[\operatorname{Sim}(w, w')\bigr] =
              \exp \langle x, x' \rangle$~\citep{Shepard19871317}.
    \item[\textbf{(Axiom 3) Manifold hypothesis.}] A quale is a probability
          density over $\mathcal{X}$, characterized by a smooth manifold.
\end{description}

\noindent
Axiom 3 turns sharing into a matter of degree. The density peaks on the manifold
and decays away from it: a work may carry a quale strongly, weakly, or hardly at
all. The manifold is the geometry of the quale, and we call it the \textit{quale
    manifold}---an idealized principal manifold, carrying the dominant structure of
the density and smooth enough to move along.

The three axioms together ask for one thing: a representation space faithful
to what people feel in a work. How that feeling is measured, and how such a
space is obtained, we leave open. We take it as given.

Current practice already reaches for such a space. The manifold hypothesis of
Axiom 3 has served machine learning for years~\citep{Bengio20131798}, and
modern embedding methods---Transformers and large language models---aim at
the very representation these axioms describe~\citep{Grand2022}.

\paragraph{Qualia spectrum.}
A quale can vary continuously---violet shades into blue, blue into green, and so
on to red---and its quale manifold moves with it. We call such a family of
manifolds a \textit{qualia spectrum} (Figure~\ref{fig:spectra}(a)). Geometry
already knows this structure as a \textit{fiber bundle}, and we call it a
\textit{spectrum fiber bundle}: the base space $\mathcal{U}$ is the
parameter---of any dimension---along which the quale varies, and each fiber is a
quale manifold.

\paragraph{Induced qualia spectrum.}
A spectrum fiber bundle yields a second spectrum. Call the given one the
\textit{base qualia spectrum}. Its fibers share a shape, so a single
coordinate system $\mathcal{V}$ fits them all, and every point of the bundle
gains an address $(u, v)$ (Figure~\ref{fig:spectra}).

Now fix $v$ and let $u$ run. This joins the points at $v$, one from every
fiber, into a manifold of its own---a \textit{section} of the bundle. Let $v$
vary, and the sections form a family. That family is itself a qualia
spectrum, brought forth by the base: the \textit{induced qualia spectrum}.
Its base space is $\mathcal{V}$, and each section is a fiber of it.

The two spectra weave one cloth, and the bundle is their product manifold
$\mathcal{U} \times \mathcal{V}$. The fibers of the base run one way, indexed
by $u$, and they are the warp: the loom holds them already. The sections run
across, indexed by $v$, and they are the weft: we thread them one by one. The
qualities are the cloth.

The warp is given, then, and the weft is ours to choose. Infinitely many
threadings fit, and the data alone cannot choose among them (panel (b)): the
ill-posedness of Section~\ref{subsec:disentanglement}~\citep{Locatello20197247}.
We need an inductive bias. Ours has two parts.

\begin{description}
    \item[\textbf{(Bias 1) Independence.}] The density over the bundle
          factorizes: $p(u, v) = p(u)\,p(v)$.
    \item[\textbf{(Bias 2) Shortest weft.}] Among such coordinate systems,
          take the one whose sections run shortest in total.
\end{description}

\noindent
Bias 1 is what makes a quale common: $v$ says the same thing wherever it sits on
the base. Bias 2 makes it faithful: by Axiom 2, a short weft joins the qualities
that feel most alike, so each section is spun from like qualities throughout.
Together the two leave one bundle---disentangled by Bias 1, taut by Bias 2.
Shortest total length is the problem of optimal transport, so we call this
inductive bias the \textit{optimal transport bias} (OT bias). The resulting
bundle is an \textit{optimal transport fiber bundle} (OT-FB), and what its
induced spectrum carries is the \textit{common qualia} of the base (panel (c)).
With $\mathcal{V}$ low-dimensional, what it keeps is principal, in the sense of
least error, as in PCA{}. Finally, we obtain the disentangled representation
(panel (d)).

\subsection{Content Space Equipped with
    Denotation}\label{subsec:denotation}

\paragraph{Denotation and connotation.}
Section~\ref{subsec:naming} divided meaning by naming. That division now falls
on qualia. A quale that has a name is a \textit{denotation}; a quale that has
none is a \textit{connotation}. Their quale manifolds we call
\textit{denotative} and \textit{connotative}.

Naming leaves the geometry untouched. Jazz-ness and ``quiet, with feeling in
it'' are manifolds of one kind, and only one of them has a word. The
catalogue records the first; $\mathcal{X}$ holds both.

\paragraph{Facet.}
A qualia spectrum that has a name is a \textit{facet}~\citep{Ranganathan1967}.
Genre is one. Its quale manifolds---jazz, classical, and the rest---are its
\textit{foci}, and together they vary with genre-ness. A catalogue gives a work
one focus. Here the foci become continuous: a work no longer takes one, but a
position among them. That position is the base coordinate $u$.


%%% Subsection 4.3 %%%
\subsection{Facet-Induced Connotation Space}\label{subsec:principal_connotative}

\paragraph{Definition.}
A facet is the base of a spectrum fiber bundle, so the construction of
Section~\ref{subsec:content_space} applies unchanged
(Figure~\ref{fig:spectra}). The foci are its fibers---jazz, classical, and the
rest---and they are the warp, already strung. Thread the weft by the two
biases, and the induced spectrum follows. Every work then sits at an address
$(u, v)$: $u$ says which focus, $v$ says which section.

What does each coordinate carry? The facet has a name, so $u$ carries a
denotation: $\mathcal{U}$ is the \textit{denotation space}, holding the one
denotation this library admits. The other coordinate $v$ carries what the facet
cannot name. Genre names jazz and classical, nothing more, yet $v$ holds a quale
common to every focus. A quale without a name is a connotation
(Section~\ref{subsec:denotation}), so $\mathcal{V}$ is the \textit{facet-induced
    connotation space}.

\paragraph{Facet-induced OT-FB.}
The bundle so built we call the \textit{facet-induced OT-FB}. It is the
multi-floor library with rabbit holes itself, now in geometry.
Section~\ref{sec:framework} postulated such a library; here it exists.

Figure~\ref{fig:two_views}(c) draws it. On the left stands the library. A
work has an address $(u, v)$: its genre-ness $u$ in $\mathcal{U}$, its
connotative coordinate $v$ in $\mathcal{V}$. The two run apart---which floor,
and where on the floor. On the right lies the content data, where an
embedding $\Phi$ sends the address to a point $\mathbf{x} = \Phi(u, v)$.
There the two no longer run apart: each floor $\Phi(u, \cdot)$ curves into a
galaxy, and each rabbit hole $\Phi(\cdot, v)$ into a curve running from
galaxy to galaxy. One representation, two views.

And it is unique. Two things come from us: the facet, and the dimensions
$D_\mathcal{U}$ and $D_\mathcal{V}$. Fix them, and the two biases leave one
bundle, essentially unique under mild regularity.
Appendix~\ref{sec:computation} gives the proof and the algorithm.

Both dimensions are ours to choose, and two is the usual answer: a space one
can walk is a plane. Set $D_\mathcal{V}$ to two, and the biases return the
$D_\mathcal{V}$-principal connotation space---the best two-dimensional
account of what the foci share. $D_\mathcal{U}$ answers to the foci, which are
usually few: one or two dimensions hold ten genres with room to spare. Give
the facet many foci, though, and $\mathcal{U}$ becomes a space to explore in
its own right. Let
the facet be artist, and $\mathcal{U}$ arranges artists by how near their
quale manifolds lie in $\mathcal{X}$; the reader may walk from artist to
artist, as well as from floor to floor.

\paragraph{What $\mathcal{V}$ holds.}
The coordinate $v$ carries a correspondence among the genres. Suppose each
genre holds a thousand works. The OT bias then matches every jazz piece to
one classical piece, one folk piece, and so on through every floor---a
thousand matched sets, chosen so that the total distance is least. Draw those
matches in $\mathcal{X}$, and a thousand near-parallel curves appear
(Figures~\ref{fig:spectra}(c) and~\ref{fig:mtg_otfb}(d)). That parallel
structure is the unnamed quale the genres share, and $v$ is the address of one
curve within it.

Say the match sends Miles Davis to Debussy. Nearness in $\mathcal{X}$ is not
what joins them. Miles Davis holds a position within jazz, Debussy a
homologous position within classical, and the match follows from that. A
rabbit hole rises from how the genres stand as wholes, rather than from any
affinity between two works.

Note what made this possible. The connotation came out of the content data, yet
the content data alone could not have yielded it. Nor could the facet alone: a
name gathers only what it names. Both were needed. The name laid a foundation,
and the foundation points toward what it cannot itself represent. Along that
direction, we look into the content data, and the library rises there. Naming
reaches its limit, and there it becomes the ground for what lies beyond.
