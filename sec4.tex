\section{The Definite Form: From Fiber Bundles to Optimal
  Transport}\label{sec:mathematics}

Section~\ref{sec:framework} proposed the library with rabbit holes: a
stack of floors, threaded by a bundle of holes. To build it, the
concept must first be given a mathematical formulation. This section
presents two objects. The first is the \textbf{optimal transport fiber
    bundle (OT-FB)}, the bundle of rabbit holes in exact form. The second
is the \textbf{principal connotation space}, spanned by the holes
themselves, which fixes what the bundle expresses and what it does not.


%%% Subsection 4.1 The Library as a Fiber Bundle %%%
\subsection{The Library as a Fiber
    Bundle}\label{subsec:library_as_bundle}

% --- (1) Setup: content data + genre label → coordinate pair ---
Let us restate what Section~\ref{sec:framework} drew. The works arrive as
content data: each work $n$ is a point $\mathbf{x}_n \in \mathcal{X} =
    \mathbb{R}^D$ in the universe of Section~\ref{subsec:multi_galaxy}, with its
genre label $c_n$ as descriptor. What the library gives each work $n$ is a pair
of coordinates $(u_n, v_n)$: a denotative coordinate $u_n \in
    \mathcal{U}=\mathbb{R}^{L_\mathcal{U}}$---which floor, its genre-ness---and a
connotative coordinate $v_n \in \mathcal{V}=\mathbb{R}^{L_\mathcal{V}}$---where
on the floor.

% --- (2) Φ: floors and rabbit holes as two ways of walking; naming: fiber bundle ---
The library and the universe are joined by an embedding map
\[
    \Phi: \mathcal{U} \times \mathcal{V} \to \mathcal{X},
\]
which carries every point of the library into the universe---not
only the points where works sit, but every point a visitor may walk
(Figure~\ref{fig:OT-FB}). The structures of
Section~\ref{sec:framework} are now two ways of walking $\Phi$. Hold
the genre and vary the connotation: $\Phi(u, \cdot)$ traces the
floor at $u$---its galaxy, which we model, as a working
assumption, as a smooth low-dimensional manifold. Hold the
connotation and vary the genre: $\Phi(\cdot, v)$ traces a rabbit
hole---a curve threading every galaxy through the points that share
$v$. A structure of exactly this kind---a family of manifolds bound
together by a bundle of curves---has a ready-made name in
mathematics: a \textit{fiber bundle}, with the denotation space
$\mathcal{U}$ as its base and the connotation space $\mathcal{V}$ as
its fiber. The library and the universe of
Figure~\ref{fig:two_views} are this one bundle seen twice: flat in
$\mathcal{U} \times \mathcal{V}$, where floors stack and holes run
straight; curved in $\mathcal{X}$, where galaxies scatter and holes
curve.

% --- (3) Three questions: modelable? unique? does v express connotation? ---
The three questions of Section~\ref{sec:framework} can now be put
exactly. First, can a given collection be modeled
by this geometry at all---does such a bundle exist over the works?
Second, if it exists, is it unique? Third, if it were made unique,
could we expect its coordinate $v$ to express connotation?

\paragraph{The two-genre case.}
\paragraph{The two-genre case.}
As the simplest case, consider a library with two genres only. In the content
space, the two are distributed in distinct regions, forming two galaxies
(Figure~\ref{fig:two_category_ot}a). To define an exploration space is to
specify a manifold, a curved surface in that space, with coordinates on it. If
the whole collection were covered by a single exploration space, that space
would express chiefly the difference between the genres
(Figure~\ref{fig:two_category_ot}b). If instead each genre is given an
exploration space of its own, each carries its own coordinate system, and the
correspondence between the two genres cannot be expressed
(Figure~\ref{fig:two_category_ot}c). What we seek is the case where each genre
has its own exploration space, yet the coordinate systems share something that
transcends genre (Figure~\ref{fig:two_category_ot}d)---and we want that shared
something to be connotation. This joining of two manifolds through one
coordinate system is the rabbit hole, or fiber.

Such a shared coordinate system is a correspondence: a rule laying one galaxy
onto the other. Finding it is known to be severely ill-posed
(Section~\ref{subsec:disentanglement}): infinitely many correspondences fit
the data equally well~\cite{Locatello20197247}. To settle on one, we must
state which we count as good---the inductive bias
Section~\ref{subsec:disentanglement} called for. In other words, we must
define what we want the connotation coordinate $v$ to express. Fortunately,
two assumptions suggest themselves naturally.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{(A1) Independence:} the connotation we want is
          information independent of the denotation---the
          rabbit hole as the library drew it, a passage running
          perpendicular to the floors.
    \item \textbf{(A2) Proximity:} works sharing a connotation lie close in
          the data space---works A and B in Figure~\ref{fig:two_category_ot}d,
          joined by one $v$, should stand as near to each other as they can.
\end{itemize}

Together, the two assumptions turn the search for rabbit holes
into a well-posed problem mathematics already knows: lay one galaxy onto the
other as closely as possible (A2) while keeping the match
independent of genre (A1). This is \textit{optimal
    transport}---carrying all the stars of one galaxy onto the other
with the least total effort, measured by squared Euclidean
distance
(2-Wasserstein)~\cite{villani2009optimal,peyre2019computational}.
For this cost, the correspondence exists and, under mild
regularity conditions, is essentially unique---a classical result
known as Brenier's theorem~\cite{Brenier1991375,villani2009optimal}.

This reformulation offers an insight. Miles Davis and Debussy are not
matched because each is the other's nearest work---matching
nearest works, pair by pair, would not even guarantee (A1). They
are matched because the relative position each occupies within
its own galaxy is homologous
(Figure~\ref{fig:two_category_ot}d). A rabbit hole is decided not
by the pairwise relation of two works but by how one genre stands
to another as a whole.


\paragraph{The $K$-genre case.}\label{subsec:OTFB-model}
A real collection has many floors, and correspondences drawn pair by pair
need not agree with one another. The extension, however, is direct. Optimal
transport generalizes from two distributions to $K$ as
\textit{multi-marginal optimal transport}~\cite{pass2015multi}: a single
correspondence threads all $K$ galaxies at once, picking one point from each
and minimizing the total squared distance among them. Because all genres now
enter one problem, the threads cannot disagree with one another; and
existence and essential uniqueness carry over under the same kind of
regularity conditions~\cite{pass2015multi}.

What this builds is the object
Section~\ref{subsec:library_as_bundle} described. Works on one
thread share one connotative coordinate $v$; with genre-ness
treated as continuous---unobserved genres filling between the
observed ones---each thread becomes a smooth curve through every
galaxy. Galaxies as manifolds, bound by a bundle of such curves:
the fiber bundle of Section~\ref{subsec:library_as_bundle}, its
correspondence no longer arbitrary but optimal. We call this
structure the \textbf{optimal transport fiber bundle
    (OT-FB)}---and it is exactly what
Figure~\ref{fig:OT-FB} has been showing all along.

\subsection{The Principal Connotation Space}\label{subsec:bundle_reach}

The connotation space the OT-FB provides does not cover every connotation. What,
then, is this space---and how far can it be relied on? Four properties define
it. (i) It runs independent of denotation: what a work owes to its genre stays
with the genre, and this space holds the rest. (ii) It keeps the closeness the
content data report: works those data place near one another are placed near
here too. (iii) Of all that remains once denotation is set aside, it holds the
principal $L_\mathcal{V}$ components, in the sense of least error. (iv) And it
is not chosen by hand: the content data determine it, essentially uniquely.
These four properties are equivalent to the orthogonal complement of the
descriptors, reduced in dimension by nonlinear PCA; that is, what should be
called the \textbf{principal connotation space}.


Theoretically, these four come from two assumptions and one constant. The first
two are (A1) and (A2) themselves; the fourth follows from them, through the
optimal transport of Section~\ref{subsec:two_genres}. The third rests on
$L_\mathcal{V}$, the dimension of $\mathcal{V}$, which we fix in advance: with
enough data it may be set large, though two dimensions are convenient enough
for exploration. And \textit{principal} carries its usual sense---as PCA
returns principal components under least squared error, the OT-FB returns them
under least 2-Wasserstein error.

We do not claim that the connotation space $\mathcal{V}$ signifies any
specific connotation. What it is, precisely, is the residual information
the descriptors could not carry, expressed as the orthogonal complement of
denotation and reduced to its principal components---nothing more, nothing
less. The two are therefore of wholly different origin, and are never
equal. Connotation has no fixed place: it moves with the person, the time,
and the context, a particle in motion through whatever space of meaning
there is. No space can be given to what moves---only the space it moves in.
Yet for a seeker after what the descriptors could not express, this
residual space is where, most likely, what he is looking for is to be
found. In this sense the coordinate $v$ is not a connotation itself, but
an unnamed vocabulary with which a connotation can be spoken: ``\textit{ah,
    that is it---that is how I feel today}.'' That is what the name
\textit{principal connotation space} signifies.

One remark before we close. The connotation coordinate is determined by the
content data, so another content dataset yields another coordinate. Give music
in two views, as audio and as lyrics, and two bundles are built: two kinds of
rabbit hole open underfoot, and each work receives the coordinates $(u,
    v_{\mathrm{audio}}, v_{\mathrm{lyrics}})$. The visitor is no longer asked
whether to leap; he is asked which hole to take.
