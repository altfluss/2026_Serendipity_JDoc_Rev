\section{Computing the OT-FB}\label{sec:computation}

In this section, we show that the OT-FB is determined essentially
uniquely by the collection data---that is, the pairs of descriptors and
content data---and describe how to compute it from an actual
collection. Readers willing to take the computation on trust may skip
to Section~\ref{sec:demonstration}.


%%% Subsection 5.1 Objective function %%%
\subsection{Derivation of the Objective Function and Uniqueness of
    Solutions}

Estimating the OT-FB means determining the model distribution
$q_\Phi(x)$, defined by the embedding map $\Phi$, from the distribution
$p_\text{data}(x)$ of the content data. The two distributions are given
by
\begin{align*}
    p_{\text{data}}(x) & = \sum_{n=1}^N p(n)\,
    \mathcal{N}(x \mid \mathbf{x}_n, \beta^{-1}I), \quad
    p(n) = \frac{1}{K N_{k(n)}},                                \\
    q_\Phi(x)          & = \iint_{\mathcal{U}\times\mathcal{V}}
    \mathcal{N}(x \mid \Phi(u, v), \beta^{-1}I)\, q(u)\, q(v)\, du\, dv,
\end{align*}
where $\beta^{-1}$ is the observation noise variance, $k(n)$ is the
genre of work $n$, and $N_k$ is the number of works in genre $k$.
Besides $\Phi$, we estimate the coordinates of the $N$ works:
$U = (u_1, \dots, u_N)$ in the denotation space and
$V = (v_1, \dots, v_N)$ in the connotation space.

Our goal is to establish the correspondence across all $K$ genres
through optimal transport---the multi-marginal problem of
Section~\ref{subsec:OTFB-model}. The learning objective is to find the
map $\Phi^*$ that best aligns the $K$ genre distributions, formalized
as minimizing the 2-Wasserstein distance:
\begin{align}\label{eq:main_objective}
    W^2_2(p_\text{data}, q_\Phi)
           & = \inf_{\gamma\in\Pi(p_{\text{data}},\, q_\Phi)}
    \iint \|x - x'\|^2\, \gamma(x, x')\, dx\, dx',            \\
    \Phi^* & = \argmin_{\Phi} W^2_2(p_\text{data}, q_\Phi),
\end{align}
where $\gamma \in \Pi$ is a transport plan satisfying the marginal
constraints $p_{\text{data}}(x) = \int \gamma(x,x')\,dx'$ (the
constraint $p \rightarrow q$) and $q_\Phi(x') = \int \gamma(x,x')\,dx$
(the constraint $q \rightarrow p$). Because $\Phi$ is constrained to
share one connotative coordinate $v$ across all genres, minimizing this
objective fits each galaxy and optimizes the correspondence between the
galaxies at once. That this correspondence is uniquely determined is
the content of the following proposition.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:OT-FB}
    Let each genre distribution $p_k$ be smoothed by the noise model
    $\mathcal{N}(x \mid \mathbf{x}_n, \beta^{-1}I)$, so that it is
    absolutely continuous with finite second moment. Then:
    (a) the multi-marginal optimal transport problem over
    $p_1, \dots, p_K$ with pairwise squared-distance cost has a unique
    solution, given by Monge maps; the OT-FB is therefore uniquely
    determined by the collection data.
    (b) The map $\Phi$ realizing this correspondence is unique up to a
    relabeling of the coordinates of $\mathcal{U}$ and $\mathcal{V}$;
    the arrangement of works is not affected.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
    (a) Each $p_k$ is a finite Gaussian mixture, hence absolutely
    continuous with finite second moment. For such marginals, the
    multi-marginal optimal transport problem with pairwise
    squared-distance cost has a unique solution, and this solution is
    given by Monge maps~\cite{GangboSwiech199823,pass2015multi}; for
    $K=2$ this is Brenier's theorem~\cite{Brenier1991375}. The optimal
    correspondence across the $K$ distributions is therefore unique,
    and the OT-FB inherits this uniqueness.
    (b) Let $g_\mathcal{U}$ and $g_\mathcal{V}$ be measure-preserving
    maps of $\mathcal{U}$ and $\mathcal{V}$, i.e., maps leaving $q(u)$
    and $q(v)$ invariant. Then $\Phi \circ (g_\mathcal{U} \times
        g_\mathcal{V})$ induces the same model distribution $q_\Phi$ and
    the same transport cost, and realizes the same correspondence.
    Conversely, any two maps realizing the optimal correspondence of
    (a) differ only by such a reparametrization.
\end{proof}

This is the exact sense of \emph{essentially unique}: what the
collection data determine is the arrangement of works itself, not the
names of the axes---which were never meant to carry names
(Section~\ref{sec:introduction}).

\subsection{Optimization of the Objective Function}

Our task is now to find the map $\Phi$ minimizing
\eqref{eq:main_objective}, together with the coordinates $(u_n, v_n)$
of every work $n$. The optimization is subject to the two marginal
constraints, and we handle them alternately: each step enforces one
constraint under one-sided (unbalanced) transport.

\paragraph{Transport optimization $q_\Phi \rightarrow p_\text{data}$.}
With $(U, V)$ fixed and the marginal constraint $q \rightarrow p$
satisfied, we optimize the map $\Phi$. The minimizer is
\begin{equation}
    \Phi(u, v \mid U, V) =
    \frac{\sum_k \mathcal{N}(u \mid u_k, \alpha^{-1} I)\, \varphi_k(v)}
    {\sum_{k'} \mathcal{N}(u \mid u_{k'}, \alpha^{-1} I)},
    \label{eq:kernel_smoothing}
\end{equation}
where $u_k$ is the floor coordinate shared by the works of genre $k$
(that is, $u_n \equiv u_{k(n)}$ under hard genre labels), and
$\varphi_k$ is the manifold model of the $k$-th genre:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:varphi}
    \varphi_k(v) =
    \frac{\sum_{n:\rho_{kn}=1} \mathcal{N}(v \mid v_n, \alpha^{-1} I)\,
        \mathbf{x}_n}
    {\sum_{n':\rho_{kn'}=1} \mathcal{N}(v \mid v_{n'}, \alpha^{-1} I)}.
\end{equation}
Here $\rho_{kn} = 1$ when work $n$ belongs to genre $k$ and $0$
otherwise, and $\alpha$ controls the kernel width. Crucially,
\eqref{eq:kernel_smoothing} takes the form of \textit{hierarchical
    kernel smoothing}---the algorithm of the SOM of
SOMs~\cite{Furukawa2009463}---arising here not as a modeling assumption
but as a consequence of optimal transport.

\paragraph{Transport optimization $p_\text{data} \rightarrow q_\Phi$.}
With $\Phi$ given by \eqref{eq:kernel_smoothing}, the objective
\eqref{eq:main_objective} under the marginal constraint
$p \rightarrow q$ becomes an objective over $(U, V)$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:E-UV}
    E[U, V] = \sum_{n=1}^N p(n)
    \iint p(u, v \mid n)\,
    \frac{\beta}{2}\bigl\|\mathbf{x}_n - \Phi(u, v \mid U, V)\bigr\|^2
    \, du\, dv
    + \lambda R(U, V),
\end{equation}
where $R$ is the regularization term
\begin{equation*}
    R(U, V) \equiv - \sum_{n=1}^N p(n)\,
    \bigl(\log q(u_n) + \log q(v_n)\bigr),
\end{equation*}
and $\lambda$ is its strength.

\paragraph{Final objective.}
Applying the Laplace approximation, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reduced_objective}
    E[U, V] \simeq \sum_{n=1}^N p(n) \left\{
    \frac{\beta}{2}\bigl\| \mathbf{x}_n - \Phi(u_n, v_n \mid U, V)
    \bigr\|^2
    - \lambda \log q(u_n)
    - \lambda \log q(v_n)
    \right\}.
\end{equation}
This reduction to a single objective over the coordinates $(U, V)$
enables efficient gradient-based optimization.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
The method admits two extensions. First, when a work carries a soft
genre distribution---Gershwin's works as jazz and classical---the hard
label $k(n)$ is replaced by that distribution: $u_n$ departs from
$u_{k(n)}$ and becomes a per-work coordinate, and $\rho_{kn}$ becomes a
fuzzy membership. Genre-ness is thus made continuous, work by work.

Second, when richer descriptors are available---artist, era, and
others---the denotation space $\mathcal{U}$ can itself be further
disentangled into independent axes: genre-ness, artist-ness, era-ness,
and so on---a catalogue's facets, made continuous. The connotation
space remains the remainder of them all.
