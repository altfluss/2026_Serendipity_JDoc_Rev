%%%%% Section 6. Demonstration with real data %%%%%
\section{Demonstration}\label{sec:demonstration}

Sections~\ref{sec:framework}--\ref{sec:mathematics} designed the
library in concept, and in mathematics. This section
builds two. The first, a music collection, takes its content data
from sound alone. The second, a collection of children's books, takes
two kinds of content data, and so opens two bundles of rabbit holes
over one base. We first state what the construction asks of the
content data, then build the two libraries in turn.


%%% Subsection 6.1 %%%
\subsection{What the Construction Asks of the Content
    Data}\label{subsec:content_data}

\paragraph{Unfolded content data.}
The construction asks one thing of the content data: that closeness
in $\mathcal{X}$ mean kinship between works. Both the proximity (A2)
and the transport cost are stated in the Euclidean distance of
$\mathcal{X}$, so what counts as close there decides which works a
rabbit hole joins. Two requirements follow.

The first is Euclidean geometry. The content data must carry an inner
product, or a distance that is Euclidean at least locally. The second
is that the space be \emph{unfolded}. A space of raw signals is
folded: kindred works may lie far apart, and local distance tells
little about kinship. Unfolding is the preprocessing the construction
asks for, and it must be generic---made for no particular task. Deep
embeddings meet both requirements: foundation encoders are trained on
large corpora so that semantic closeness becomes geometric closeness,
and trained for no single purpose.

\paragraph{Description as content data.}
A description of a work, embedded by a large language model (LLM),
meets both requirements. An abstract or a synopsis serves as it
stands. What the embedding returns is no longer a set of words but a
place, and the place keeps the nuance: \textit{chill} and
\textit{relaxed} land close, as they do in use, and \textit{beautiful
    and sad} is read as one impression rather than two labels.

Where no description exists, one can be composed from the tags users
have freely given---``\textit{this work is described by the following
    words: $t_1, t_2, \ldots$}''---and embedded whole. No controlled
vocabulary is needed; whatever words come will do.

Which words are embedded then decides what the bundle carries. Embed
the nouns given to a work, and its themes enter the content space;
embed the adjectives, and the impression it leaves does.
Section~\ref{subsec:book_library} uses this freedom to give one
collection two kinds of rabbit holes.

\subsection{A Music Library Built from Sound
    Alone}\label{subsec:music_library}

\paragraph{Setup.}
The first library is built from MTG-Jamendo~\cite{Bogdanov2019}, an
openly licensed music collection. Five genres---classical, jazz, rock,
electronic, and folk---enter the library, one thousand tracks each, and
every track carries exactly one genre tag. The content data come from
sound alone: each track is embedded by CLAP~\cite{Wu2023}, a joint
audio--text encoder, over excerpts drawn from across it, then averaged,
normalized to unit norm, and reduced by PCA to ten dimensions. These
embeddings and the genre labels are all the modeling uses. The
collection also carries community tags---instruments, moods, and
themes---and they are held out, to be read afterward against the space
the sound itself has built.

\paragraph{The learned bundle.}
Figure~\ref{fig:mtg_otfb} shows the collection modeled by the OT-FB.
The five galaxies separate cleanly in the content space
$\mathcal{X}$: sound alone, embedded, already tells the genres apart.
In (a) a manifold is fitted to each galaxy independently; in (b) the
five are learned together as one OT-FB. The fit is much the same, but
the manifolds of (b) are floors of one bundle.

Panels (c) and (d) stack the manifolds apart as floors, each line
joining the same $v$ through all five. In (c) the lines cross and
tangle: every manifold carries a coordinate of its own. In (d) the
same lines descend in order. Five galaxies that lie apart in
$\mathcal{X}$ now share one coordinate system, and each line is a
rabbit hole: five works, one per genre, standing at the same $v$.

Both results satisfy the independence (A1): in (c) as in (d), $v$ runs
independently of the genre. (A1) alone leaves the floors unaligned.
The proximity (A2) aligns them, minimizing the total length of the
connections between the manifolds.

\paragraph{Reading $\mathcal{V}$ through the tags.}
What does the connotation space $\mathcal{V}$ hold? The tags held out
of the modeling---instruments, moods, and themes---give the reading.
Each tag defines a density over $\mathcal{V}$: the distribution
$p(v \mid \textit{tag})$ of the works that carry it.
Figure~\ref{fig:v_tags}(a--d) shows four of them---\textit{piano},
\textit{trumpet}, \textit{acoustic guitar}, and \textit{happy}. Each
peaks at a place of its own, yet the four densities
overlap---connotation runs as a continuous spectrum, not as discrete
regions.

Tags differ in how much they tell about $v$. The mutual information
$\mathrm{MI}[v; \textit{tag}]$ measures it: how unevenly the works
carrying the tag fall over $\mathcal{V}$. Table~\ref{tab:tag_mi}
lists the top twenty. Instruments occupy the upper ranks, and moods
follow.

\paragraph{The landscape of $\mathcal{V}$.}
Figure~\ref{fig:v_tags}(e) widens the reading to the top forty tags,
each placed at the peak of its density $p(v \mid \textit{tag})$. Each
tag still spreads around its peak, as (a--d) show; the map marks
where it gathers most.

The landscape divides in two. The upper half is lively: to the right,
drums, bass, electric guitar, and brass---powerful music; to the
left, happy and uplifting. The lower half is quiet: to the right,
acoustic guitar, with love and dream---ballads; to the left, piano,
with emotional, relaxing, sad, and romantic. The two coordinates of
$\mathcal{V}$ carry no names, no more than a principal component
does; yet the landscape they span is legible from end to end.

Table~\ref{tab:point_tags} reads four points more closely: the
corners A--D, each a rabbit hole, seen through the tags concentrated
there. At A the music is fast and energetic, played by brass and
bongo; at B, love ballads on acoustic guitar; at C, funny songs on
accordion and ukulele; at D, piano, relaxing and sad. No single tag
names a point; the tags gathered there do.

\paragraph{What $\mathcal{V}$ represents.}
The tags have read $\mathcal{V}$ as a continuous spectrum.
Connotation itself, though, is known by hearing rather than by words.
It lies in what the works gathered at one point have in common, and no
single word holds it. The tags cannot name it; they let the seeker
guess what a point holds, and a guess is enough to start walking.

$\mathcal{V}$ does not hold every connotation---only the principal
ones. Here it is built from the sound and the genre labels alone:
genre gave the denotation, and all else was left to connotation. Of
that remainder $\mathcal{V}$ keeps the principal components, in the
sense of least error (Section~\ref{subsec:content_space}). They read as
instruments first, and the moods that go with them
(Table~\ref{tab:tag_mi}). \textit{Principal} claims no more than this:
many connotations run through the music, and $\mathcal{V}$ holds those
that account for it best.

A single point of $\mathcal{V}$ does not hold a single kind of music.
The works gathered there are alike in connotation, yet they run from
genre to genre; the space is one, shared by the five. Each point is a
rabbit hole, opening at the same $v$ on every floor. At point A
(Figure~\ref{fig:v_tags}), rock is energetic music driven by drums;
through the hole, on the classical floor, brass plays with the same
force. At point D, emotional piano jazz leads to romantic classical
piano---and to some trumpet as well, faint but there (panel~(b))---
close to what the visitor of Section~\ref{sec:introduction} was
seeking.

\paragraph{Setup.}
The second library is built from 409 well-known children's books. An
LLM annotator (OpenAI GPT-4) gives each book two descriptions: six to
eight nouns for its themes, and six to eight adjectives for the
impression it leaves. Across the collection, 74 nouns and 79
adjectives are used. Each set is joined into a description as in
Section~\ref{subsec:content_data}---``\textit{This is a children's
    book. The impressions are humorous, warm, and adventurous}''---then
embedded by an LLM text encoder (OpenAI
\texttt{text-embedding-3-small}) and prepared as in
Section~\ref{subsec:music_library}. One collection thus yields two
content datasets: theme data and impression data.

The descriptors are fifteen genres, assigned by the same annotator.
They arrive fuzzy: each book names a primary genre, a secondary,
sometimes a third, each with a strength---fantasy above all, adventure
next. Genre-ness is thus continuous, and the floor coordinate becomes
a per-work coordinate $u_n$: the case the first extension of
Section~\ref{sec:computation} anticipated.

\paragraph{One base, two bundles.}
The base is built first, and both bundles rest on it. The genre
descriptors, verbalized with their strengths and embedded likewise,
give each book its denotation coordinate $u_n$.
Figure~\ref{fig:book_otfb}(a) shows the resulting genre map: the two
dramas side by side, the two fantasies adjoining legends, myths, and
folktales, adventure beside science fiction and horror. Two bundles
are then learned over this one base, one from the theme data, one from
the impression data. Every book now holds one denotative address and
two connotative ones, $(u, v_{\mathrm{thm}}, v_{\mathrm{imp}})$: under
every book, two rabbit holes open.

\paragraph{The two landscapes.}
Figures~\ref{fig:book_otfb}(b) and (c) are read as $\mathcal{V}$ was
in Section~\ref{subsec:music_library}. The theme landscape divides
into bonds and inner life in the upper left---\textit{family},
\textit{friendship}, \textit{loss}; struggle in the upper
right---\textit{war}, \textit{courage}, \textit{oppression}; the play
of the mind in the lower left---\textit{imagination},
\textit{curiosity}, \textit{discovery}. The impression landscape
divides along other lines: sorrow above---\textit{melancholic},
\textit{poignant}, \textit{haunting}; warmth in the upper
right---\textit{gentle}, \textit{cozy}, \textit{nostalgic}; play in
the lower right---\textit{whimsical}, \textit{playful},
\textit{humorous}. The same 409 books are arranged twice: $u$ carries
the genre; $v_{\mathrm{thm}}$ and $v_{\mathrm{imp}}$ carry two
connotations---what a book is about, and the impression it leaves.

\paragraph{Which hole to take.}
Table~\ref{tab:book_holes} takes the leap. Three books stand on the
same floor, Drama-Daily, and under each the two holes open. Through a
theme hole, the books met on other floors share the subject of the
book left behind; through an impression hole, they share the
impression it leaves. A leap is not the end: hopping off, the visitor
walks the new floor as before, among the books gathered around the
hole. The same book, two holes, two journeys.

\bigskip\noindent
The remark that closed Section~\ref{sec:framework} is now an
actual library. The visitor is no longer asked whether to leap; he
is asked which hole to take.
